Proposition 1.3.5.

If \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) is a convergent series, then the sequence \(\{a_n\}_{n=1} ^\infty\) must converge to \(0\text{.}\)
🔗
In other words, a necessary but not sufficient condition for a series to converge is that its underlying sequence must converge to \(0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Suppose \(\sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) converges to \(L\text{.}\) Let \(\epsilon \gt 0\text{.}\) Then there exists \(N\) such that
\begin{equation*} L-\dfrac{\epsilon}{2} \lt \sum _{n=1} ^k a_n \lt L+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2} \end{equation*}
for all \(k \gt N\text{.}\) Then for all \(k \gt N\text{,}\) we have
\begin{equation*} |a_{k+1}|=|\sum _{n=1}^{k+1} a_n - \sum _{n=1} ^k a_n| \lt \epsilon\text{.} \end{equation*}
Thus the sequence \(\{a_n\} _{n=1} ^\infty\) converges to \(0\text{.}\)
