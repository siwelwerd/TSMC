Lemma 1.7.10.

Let \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=0} ^\infty a_n (x-c)^n\) be a power series. If the series converges at \(x=r\text{,}\) then it converges absolutely on the interval \(|x-c| \lt |r-c|\text{,}\) i.e. at all points closer to \(c\) than \(r\) is.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
By making a substitution \(y=x-c\text{,}\) we may assume without loss of generality that \(c=0\text{.}\) Assume further without loss of generality that \(r \gt 0\text{,}\) and let \(-r \lt b \lt r\) be given. By the root test, since \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=0} ^\infty a_n r^n\) converges, we must have \(|a_nr^n|^\frac{1}{n} \leq 1\) (since if bigger than 1, the series would diverge by the root test). Then we compute
\begin{equation*} |a_nb^n|^\frac{1}{n}=b|a_n|^\frac{1}{n} \lt |r||a_n|^\frac{1}{n} \leq 1 \end{equation*}
Thus we see by the root test that \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n b^n\) converges absolutely.
