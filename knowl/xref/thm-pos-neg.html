Theorem 1.5.8.

A series \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) is absolutely convergent if and only if \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^\infty a_n = L\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^\infty a_n = M\text{,}\) in which case \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty |a_n|=L-M\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Suppose \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) is absolutely convergent, i.e. \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty |a_n|=c\text{.}\) Then every partial sum of positive terms satisfies \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^k a_n \leq c\text{,}\) so the sequence of partial sums is a bounded increasing sequence and hence convergent. Similarly, noting that for negative terms \(|a_n|=-a_n\text{,}\) we have \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^k -a_n \leq c\text{,}\) so \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^k a_n \geq -c\text{.}\) Thus the sequence of partial sums of negative terms is a bounded decreasing sequence and hence convergent.
🔗
Suppose conversely that \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^\infty a_n = L\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^\infty a_n = M\text{.}\) Then
\begin{align*} \sum _{n=1} ^\infty |a_n| \amp = \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^\infty |a_n|+\sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^\infty |a_n| \\ \amp = \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^\infty a_n+\sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^\infty -a_n \\ \amp = L - M \end{align*}
