    Section 1.4 Comparison Tests

    In proving that \(p\)-series converge in Section 1.3, we implicitly made use of the following.
    🔗

    Theorem 1.4.1. Comparison Test.

    Let \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) be two series.
    • If \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) converges and \(0 \leq a_n \leq b_n\) for all \(n\text{,}\) then \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) also converges.
      🔗
    • If \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) diverges and \(a_n \geq b_n \geq 0\) for all \(n\text{,}\) then \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) also diverges.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    First, suppose \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) converges to \(S\) and \(0 \leq a_n \leq b_n\) for all \(n\text{.}\) Then for every \(k\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \sum _{n=1} ^k a_n \leq \sum _{n=1} ^k b_n \leq S\text{,} \end{equation*}
    so the series \(\sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) is a bounded, monotonic sequence and hence converges.
    🔗
    Now suppose instead \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) diverges and \(a_n \geq b_n \geq 0\) for all \(n\text{.}\) Suppose \(L\) is any real number. Then since \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) is increasing and divergent, there exists \(k\) such that \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^k b_n \gt L\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^k a_n \geq \sum _{n=1} ^k b_n \gt L\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Since the series gets larger than any real number, we see that \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^k a_n\) diverges as well.
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 1.4.2.

    Because the first finitely many terms don’t affect whether a sequence or series converges or not, the comparison test can be strengthened: it is enough to know that eventually \(a_n \leq b_n\) (i.e. there exists \(N\) such that \(a_n \leq b_n\) for all \(n\gt N\)).
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.4.3.

    Consider the series \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{1}{n-3}\text{.}\) Since \(n-3 \lt n\text{,}\) \(\frac{1}{n-3} \gt \frac{1}{n}\text{.}\) Then since the harmonic series diverges, the series \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{1}{n-3}\) diverges as well.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.4.4.

    Consider the series \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{1}{n^2+5}\text{.}\) Since \(n^2+5 \gt n^2\text{,}\) \(\frac{1}{n^2+5} \lt \frac{1}{n^2}\text{.}\) Since this \(p\)-series converges, we have \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{1}{n^2+5}\) must also converge.
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 1.4.6.

    As we saw in the last part of the previous activity, direct comparison is not always effective, even if qualitatively one series seems to behave like another.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 1.4.7. Limit Comparison Test.

    Let \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) be two series with \(a_n \gt 0\) and \(b_n \gt 0\) for all \(n\text{.}\) Suppose that \(\displaystyle \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{a_n}{b_n}=c\) for some \(c \gt 0\text{.}\) Then either \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) both converge, or they both diverge.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By the definition of the limit of a sequence, there exists \(N\) such that for \(n \geq N\text{,}\) \(\dfrac{c}{2} \lt \dfrac{a_n}{b_n} \lt \dfrac{3c}{2}\text{.}\) If \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) diverges, then we have \(a_n \gt \left(\dfrac{c}{2}\right)b_n\text{,}\) so \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) also diverges by the direct comparison test. If instead \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty b_n\) converges, then \(a_n \lt \left(\dfrac{3c}{2}\right)b_n\text{,}\) so \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) converges by direct comparison test.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.4.8.

    Since \(\displaystyle \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{n^2}{n^2-n+1}=1\) and the \(p\)-series \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{1}{n^2}\) converges, by the limit comparison test we know that \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{1}{n^2-n+1}\) also converges.
    🔗
