Corollary 1.5.9.

If a series \(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty a_n\) converges but is not absolutely convergent, then both \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^\infty a_n\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^\infty a_n \) diverge.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The observation is that if a series converges, either \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \gt 0}} ^\infty a_n\) and \(\displaystyle \sum _{\substack{n=1 \\ a_n \lt 0}} ^\infty a_n \) both converge or they both diverge. By Theorem 1.5.8, absolute convergence is equivalent to both converging, so since the series is not absolutely convergent we see that they must instead both diverge.
