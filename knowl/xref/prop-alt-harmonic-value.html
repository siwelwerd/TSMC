Proposition 1.5.2.

\(\displaystyle \sum _{n=1} ^\infty \dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n} = \ln(2)\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
First recall that \(\left\{1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n}-\ln(n)\right\}_{n=1} ^\infty\) converges to a number \(\gamma\text{.}\)
🔗
Now consider the partial sum
\begin{align*} S_{2n} \amp = \dfrac{1}{1}-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n-1}+\dfrac{1}{2n} \\ \amp = \left( \dfrac{1}{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right) \\ \amp = \left( \dfrac{1}{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1}{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n}\right) \\ \amp = \left( \dfrac{1}{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2n} -\ln(2n) \right) + \ln(2n) \\ \amp \phantom{=,,}-\left(\dfrac{1}{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{n} - \ln(n) \right) - \ln(n) \end{align*}
Then we see \(\displaystyle \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} S_{2n} = \gamma - \gamma + \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \left(\ln(2n)-\ln(n)\right)=\ln(2)\)
🔗
Since we already know the alternating harmonic series converges, and we found a subsequence converging to \(\ln(2)\text{,}\) by Theorem 1.2.17 the alternating harmonic series must also converge to \(\ln(2)\text{.}\)
